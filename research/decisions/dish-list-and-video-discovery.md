# From a recipe database to a closed dish list and on-request videos

Why this version of the app has no recipe database, how dishes and videos are chosen, and what each choice rests on. Each row links to the study that holds the data, criteria and limits.

## Decision chain

| Step | Decision | Evidence | Study |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | No recipe database: recipe sites could not be used for a store release. The server keeps a closed list of 128 student-cooking dish names (130 until 2026-11-19; egg salad and kimchi porridge were removed because students living alone rarely cook them and no usable videos were found) (`server/app/dishes/dishes.json`) and shows YouTube videos instead of recipe text. | Constraint, not measurement | - |
| 2 | First design (hybrid): the server computes have/missing from key ingredients and a substitution table; the model only ranks. | 48 frozen cases (31 suggestion, 17 substitution), 14 thresholds fixed before the run: 12 met, 2 not (one off-list dish for a one-ingredient input; a wording check on a substitute sentence). Run 2 without a fixed card count: 7 model answers did not finish within 1,024 output tokens (suspected cause) and fell back to the rules list. | [dish_eval](../experiments/dish_eval/README.md) |
| 3 | Naive design: the model gets only the dish names and the user's ingredients and returns dishes with have/missing; the server checks names and nothing else. No rules fallback (502 instead). | 9 thresholds, 3 met: names in the list 99.7%, `have` all from the user's list 94.6%, recall/precision against a rules-built reference 33% / 32%. The reference is not human-labelled and favours the rules design. Chosen for simplicity and because it keeps the closed list as the only shared data. | [dish_naive](../experiments/dish_naive/README.md) |
| 4 | Output cap 4096 tokens. An answer cut at the cap keeps its complete dishes (the list is best first); found on the first phone run, where a common input of four ingredients was cut at 4,016 tokens in 3 of 4 calls, which a 502 would have shown as an error. A prompt that defines `have` as "only what the user listed" removes false `have` entries (100% vs 95.8%) but was not adopted: it did not meet the pre-set rule (cut answers counted as empty). | Cost per call is about $0.0005 and 80% of it is output; compact formats, no `have`, or "stop at poor fits" were not cheaper. 1–2 of 27 inputs still hit 8,192 tokens because the model may list the whole dish list. | [dish_tokens](../experiments/dish_tokens/README.md), [dish_naive](../experiments/dish_naive/README.md) |
| 5 | Videos are found when the user opens a dish: one web-search model call proposes links, one batched YouTube lookup keeps those that exist, are public and embeddable, in the model's order. | No pipeline variant met the pre-set rule (links for 90% of dishes within 6 s); one call per dish was the closest (83%), batching several dishes in one call gave links for 15% of dishes. Asking for ids alone returned invented ids; titles with ids worked. On-request loading was chosen to pay the search cost only for dishes the user opens (about $0.008 each). | [dish_pipeline](../experiments/dish_pipeline/README.md) |
| 6 | No title filter and no ranking by views. | The first discovery study kept the search API with a title filter and was superseded: its relevance metric was the same string rule. With 60 hand-labelled videos the title rule found 57% of relevant videos and none of the 19 with English or nameless titles. Model judges (kappa 0.61) rated 76% of the embeddable model-provided links as relevant. | [youtube_discovery](../experiments/youtube_discovery/README.md) (superseded), [youtube_discovery_v2](../experiments/youtube_discovery_v2/README.md), [dish_pipeline](../experiments/dish_pipeline/README.md) |
| 7 | Request wording D0: "videos to follow along and cook the dish". Adding "for someone cooking alone", simple-tools conditions, or exclusions did not help. | 27 dishes, two judges: relevant share 82.4% (D0) vs 75.7–77.7% (D1, D2) and 75.9% (D3, which also lowered links per dish); differences are within the bootstrap interval. The word 자취생 did not act as a search keyword. | [dish_pipeline](../experiments/dish_pipeline/README.md) (round 3) |
| 8 | **Adopted (2026-11-19): closed lists built per request.** `name` from the dish list, `have` from the user's own items, `missing` from the key ingredients the user does not own (spelling and synonym groups); the server re-checks the answer against the same lists. **Not adopted:** a model mapping of unmatched user items (다진마늘 → 마늘) to the ingredient list. | Free text vs enums: enums were obeyed on this provider (no value outside the lists in 5,000+ items); raw `response_format` worked, function calls and LangChain did not. With the whole ingredient list as choices, 8.3% of dishes listed an owned item as missing; per-request choices made it 0% on the 27 frozen inputs (paired difference −12.2 pp, 95% interval −23.3 to −3.4) with recall/precision unchanged (intervals include 0), at about one third more output (+$0.0001 and +1 s per call). The model mapping got 21 of the 40 unambiguous unmatched items right and merged 14 of the 16 traps into another ingredient (쪽파 → 대파, 두유 → 두부, 들기름 → 참기름), which would show missing ingredients as owned. | [dish_output_formats](../experiments/dish_output_formats/README.md) (rounds 1–2), [ADR 005](adr-005-closed-answer-lists.md) |
| 9 | Videos stored per dish are served only after one batched lookup confirms that they still exist, are public and can be embedded; entries of 30 days or more are ignored. | No model call for stored dishes; a deleted or private video leaves the list at the next request. | [ADR 003](adr-003-video-store-policy.md) |
| 10 | Recipes exist only where a caption transcript was available: an AI restructures it, a validator checks it against the transcript, and the server revises it to the fridge with one model call plus a rule check. | 16 recipes kept (18 validated from 22 Korean transcripts; 2 dropped because the video did not show the dish); Round 1 (36 synthetic cases) showed two gaps in the check (one-character names, main ingredient preserved); round 2 added a refusal before the call for ingredients the dish is named after and a main-ingredient rule after it: wrong recipes accepted 9 → 1 of 28 refuse cases (most were rice dishes without rice among the real recipes), no wrongful refusal, 23 of 64 model calls saved. The caption route is unofficial, was blocked after 31 requests, and its legal status is open. | [dish_revision](../experiments/dish_revision/README.md), [dish_recipes](../experiments/dish_recipes/README.md), [ADR 004](adr-004-recipe-and-revision-check.md) |

Architecture: [ADR 001](adr-001-roles-and-composition-root.md) (roles and one composition root), [ADR 002](adr-002-one-factory-for-providers.md) (one factory for providers) and [ADR 005](adr-005-closed-answer-lists.md) (closed answer lists).

## Limits that apply to all rows

- One labeller (an AI) made the answer keys and the 60 video labels; no human labels exist. Judge agreement with those labels is 0.61 (kappa), just above the pre-set 0.6.
- One model family and one run per variant; web search results change from day to day.
- The 27 dishes of the video studies contain many fried-rice dishes.
- "Easy for a person living alone" is not measured by any metric.
- The frozen cases, thresholds and criteria were committed before their results; later changes are recorded as dated amendments in each study's `criteria` file and README.
